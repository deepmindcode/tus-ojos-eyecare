# Signs a child cannot see well that parents often miss

A child who has never seen well does not know their vision is poor. They have nothing to compare it to, so they do not complain. What they do is change their behavior, and that is where you notice it.

### At home

* Holding books, the tablet or the phone unusually close.
* Rubbing their eyes while reading or doing homework, not just when tired.
* Tilting their head or closing one eye to look at something far away.
* Complaining of headaches or forehead fatigue at the end of the school day.
* Blinking a lot or watery eyes when concentrating.

### At school

* Skipping lines when reading, or tracking the line with a finger.
* Reading aloud correctly but not understanding it: the effort went into decoding the letters.
* Trouble copying from the board.
* Avoiding reading, or tiring much sooner than classmates.
* A drop in performance with no clear reason.

### Why a school screening is not always enough

School screenings mostly measure distance vision with a chart a few meters away. It is a quick, useful test, but it leaves out things that matter for reading: sustained close focusing, and whether both eyes work together. A child can pass a school screening and still struggle to read.

### What to expect at the visit

It takes 30 to 40 minutes and is paced for the child. If they do not know their letters yet, we use shapes and symbols they recognize; the measurement is just as accurate. We check how much they see, how they focus up close, how the two eyes coordinate, and the general health of the eye. At the end we explain what we found and what makes sense to do.
