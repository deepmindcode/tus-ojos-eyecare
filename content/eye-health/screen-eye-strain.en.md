# Screen eye strain: what actually helps

Hours in front of a screen tire your eyes. It does not cause permanent damage, but the fatigue is real and has specific causes.

### What is happening

Three things at once. First, you blink less: looking at a screen your blink rate drops considerably, and the eye dries out. Second, you hold focus at one close distance for hours, and the focusing muscle fatigues. Third, posture: a screen too high, too close or poorly lit forces your neck and eyes to compensate.

### What helps

* **Regular breaks.** Every twenty minutes, look at something far away for a few seconds. It sounds minor and it is what helps most.
* **Blink on purpose.** Especially if you wear contacts or already feel dryness.
* **Position the screen well.** About an arm's length away, with the center slightly below eye level.
* **Even out the light.** The screen should not be the only lit thing in a dark room, nor compete with a window behind you.
* **Check your prescription.** An out-of-date prescription, even slightly, multiplies the effort of focusing.

### What makes less difference

Blue light gets a lot of attention. The evidence that filtering it reduces fatigue is weak; what is clear is that dryness and sustained focusing explain most of the tiredness. If someone sells you a filter as the solution, that is worth knowing.

### When to get it checked

* The fatigue does not improve with breaks and better posture.
* Vision blurs when you shift from the screen to something distant, and takes a while to clear.
* You get headaches most days when you finish working.
* Your eyes feel dry, gritty or burning consistently.
