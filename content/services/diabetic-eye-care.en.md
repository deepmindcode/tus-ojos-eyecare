# Diabetic eye care

If you have diabetes, the annual eye exam is not general advice: it is part of managing your condition.

### Why it matters

Diabetes affects small blood vessels, and the retina is made of small blood vessels. Over time, high glucose levels can damage them.

The important part: that damage begins **without symptoms**. It does not hurt or itch, and vision stays good for a considerable time. That is why the exam does not wait for you to notice something.

### What to expect

1. **Your history,** including how long you have had diabetes and how your control is going.
2. **Your prescription,** which in some people fluctuates with glucose levels.
3. **An evaluation of the back of the eye,** to view the retina directly. It does not hurt.
4. **Dilating drops,** depending on the case, for a better view. If used, your vision stays blurry for a couple of hours: bring someone along or plan not to drive.
5. **An explanation of what we found,** and when to come back.

### What you can do

* An eye exam every year, even if your vision is perfect.
* Keep your glucose under control: that is what protects the retina most.
* Manage blood pressure and cholesterol too.
* Do not smoke.
* Tell us you have diabetes when you book, so we can plan the time.

### When not to wait

Call before your annual check if you notice blurry vision that comes and goes, new floating spots or strands, dark areas in your field of vision, or if one eye sees worse than the other.

### Coordinated with your doctor

This check does not replace your primary care doctor or endocrinologist: it works alongside them. We are glad to share what we find so your care moves in one direction.
