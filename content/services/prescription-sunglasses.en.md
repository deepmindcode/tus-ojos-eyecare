# Prescription sunglasses

Squinting the whole drive is not protection. If you wear glasses, your prescription can go into a sun lens too.

### What we offer

* **Your prescription in a sun lens,** single vision or progressive.
* **Full ultraviolet protection,** which is the only truly essential part.
* **Polarized lenses,** which cut glare off asphalt, water and snow.
* **High-end sunglasses,** if you are looking for a particular brand.
* **Photochromic lenses,** if you prefer one pair that adapts.

### What to look for

**Protection is on the label, not in the tint.** It should say "100% UV" or "UV400". A very dark lens without a filter protects less than a light one that has it, and it is actually worse: the dark glass makes the pupil open wider and lets more radiation in.

**Coverage matters.** A wide or wrapped frame lets less light in from the sides.

**Polarization is comfort, not protection.** It cuts glare while driving, but it does not replace the ultraviolet filter.

### When it matters most

Midday, near water, in snow, and driving into the sun. Winter too: the sun sits lower and hits straight at eye level.

### Frequently asked questions

**Can I put my prescription into sunglasses I already own?**
Sometimes. It depends on the frame and the curvature of the lens. Bring them in and we will look.

**Is this worth it for children?**
Yes, and it matters: they spend more hours outdoors than adults, and exposure adds up.

**Progressives in a sun lens?**
Yes. It is the comfortable option if you wear progressives daily and do not want to switch glasses when you step outside.
